## Sources and scope

- Current march order: user-confirmed August 14, 2026.
- Ratings: [Wyrmtable public max-level tier list](https://wyrmtable.com/tier-list), accessed August 14, 2026.
- Wyrmtable version: v3.0.

Wyrmtable's public list evaluates every dragon at max level. It places each dragon in the strongest three-dragon army it can appear in, then scores that dragon's contribution. The ranks below therefore compare **individual max-level ceilings**; they are not official grades for these exact ten lineups.

Account levels, stars, habit ranks, troop affinity, positions, and the opponent can all change which march is strongest today. Use Wyrmtable's Personal list, Army Review, or War Room for that account-specific comparison.

## What changed from the previous five-march plan

### March 1 gains Venator and becomes the clearest investment core

The former Shieldbearer core was Rhysarion / Vhagar / Malachite. Moving Venator into the current Malachite / Vhagar / Venator formation replaces Wyrmtable's #24 B-tier Rhysarion with its #1 S-tier dragon.

That gives March 1 one elite anchor and two top-11 A-tier partners. On public max-level quality, it is now the strongest and most complete trio in the current set.

### March 3 becomes a Caraxes-led development march

The earlier Vaeldra / Venator / Caraxes trio paired two of Wyrmtable's top three dragons. The new Vaeldra / Rhysarion / Caraxes formation keeps #3 Caraxes but replaces #1 Venator with #24 Rhysarion.

March 3 still has a high-end anchor, but it should no longer be treated as the automatic future #1 march on public rank alone.

### March 4 retains the highest two-dragon ceiling

Kalspire (#2 S) and Sunfyre (#5 S) remain elite. Shadowsong (#25 C) is the clear weak link. If a future replacement fits the troop affinity, position, and role, this is the first trio where one substitution could materially raise the whole march's ceiling.

### March 2 remains the balanced practical core

Zivern / Syrax / Crimson has no S-tier dragon, but also no C-, D-, or F-tier liability. Zivern's documented 5-star development can make this march stronger on the current account than its max-level public ranks suggest.

### March 5 remains good depth

Daemoros / Seasmoke / Tashix contains one A-tier and two B-tier dragons. It lacks an elite anchor, but it also avoids the severe weaknesses visible in several lower marches.

## Habit strategy update

Concentrate scarce habit materials instead of distributing them across all 30 dragons.

### Long-term core targets

The highest-ranked dragons in the ten marches are:

**Venator (#1) → Kalspire (#2) → Caraxes (#3) → Sunfyre (#5) → Crimson (#7) → Vhagar (#9) → Syrax (#10) → Malachite (#11) → Seasmoke (#12) → Tairax (#14).**

As their habits unlock and the required rarity and breed materials become available, these dragons should increasingly receive resources ahead of lower-ceiling substitutes.

### Preserve account-value exceptions

- Zivern remains an immediate-value exception because the repository's July 13 snapshot confirms 5 stars and two unlocked habits.
- Malachite's Forest's Instinct remains useful to the current top march, but upgrade it only when the full Legendary Core and Breedmark cost can be paid.
- Do not pour resources into Rhysarion or Shadowsong merely because they are already developed; their public max-level ceilings are #24 B and #25 C.
- Thunderstrike's habit line is better than his #36 F-tier overall rank suggests. Battle Rush can receive moderate investment if March 8 is actively used, but Thunderstrike should not displace the core Legendary priorities.

## Lower-march decisions

- March 9 is the most coherent rank-only depth march among 6–10 because Velar and Shadowrend are both B tier.
- March 10 has the best individual lower-march anchor in Tairax (#14 A), but Shimmer (#34 D) and Nyrena (#27 C) limit the trio.
- March 8 should be treated as Feskar-led depth. Thunderstrike is a habit-value specialist, not a high-ceiling dragon.
- March 7 lacks an A- or S-tier anchor.
- March 6 should receive the least scarce investment unless account-specific Army Review results reveal a synergy the public list cannot capture.
