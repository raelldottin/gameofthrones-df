# Current 10 Marches — Roster-Optimized September 3, 2026

## Method

This plan is intentionally recomputed from the September 3 roster state rather than from earlier battle outcomes.

**Historical battle reports are excluded from the optimization.** They remain useful as records of what happened at older account states, but they are not evidence that an older trio is still optimal after star and habit progression.

Priority order used here:

1. Current star rank and unlocked habit ranks supplied September 3.
2. Current three-dragon kit interaction: damage amplification, control, Recovery, Protect, Taunt, debuffs, cleanse and mitigation.
3. Full positive troop affinity for all three dragons.
4. Current public dragon baseline from Wyrmtable v3.0, used as a ceiling/reference rather than as the final account ranking.
5. Positioning so the Vanguard skill benefits the intended flank or damage dealer.

Wyrmtable's public max-level tier list currently places Venator, Kalspire, Caraxes, Sunfyre and Sheepstealer in S tier; Vermithor, Malachite, Crimson, Syrax, Vhagar, Seasmoke, Vermax and Tairax are among the user's A-tier options. That baseline is not allowed to erase the user's current development: Zivern, Jagadrix, Shimmer, Dawnseeker and Thunderstrike receive extra practical value here because their current star/habit investment is materially higher.

## Why the top five are ordered this way

### March 1 — Malachite / Vermithor / Venator

This is the best damage-survival balance in the current roster. Malachite is now 3★ with Forest's Instinct at rank 3 and already brings repeated team Recovery through Warden's Rally. Vermithor is purpose-built as a frontline protector: Bronze Fury can Protect the ally with the fewest troops, and Noble Sacrifice suppresses enemy Physical and Fire output even though it asks Vermithor to accept extra Tactical damage. Venator then uses the breathing room as the dedicated Physical finisher.

Vermithor takes Vanguard because his current kit is explicitly designed to anchor the frontline. Venator goes right so Vermithor's Vanguard damage-reduction support protects the main finisher.

### March 2 — Zivern / Vhagar / Seasmoke

Zivern's 6★ state is too developed to treat as an ordinary B-tier dragon: Battle Mastery 2, Keen Instinct 2 and Fearsome Reach 1 give this march substantially more current-account value than a max-level-only ranking suggests. Vhagar provides the survival layer through Taunt and takes Vanguard for its own damage reduction. Zivern sits left, where Vhagar's Vanguard support improves the flank's Tactical output. Seasmoke removes enemy positive effects and supplies large Fire hits.

The result is a real control-to-damage loop: Vhagar diverts attacks, Zivern pressures Tactical defenses, and Seasmoke prevents enemy buffs from stabilizing the fight.

### March 3 — Jagadrix / Dawnseeker / Crimson

This march exists because of the new habit data. Jagadrix is 6★ with Whispering Sabotage at rank 4, while Dawnseeker has Tactical Inferno at rank 5. Those investments are too large to bury in a lower march merely because both dragons have modest public max-level ranks.

Dawnseeker takes Vanguard because its Command supplies Recovery to adjacent allies and its Vanguard position supports the Fire-focused left flank. Jagadrix's repeated Initiative/Instinct pressure makes enemy timing worse before its Fire hits land. Crimson adds odd-round Stun chances and heavy Fire burst.

### March 4 — Sunfyre / Kalspire / Sheepstealer

This is the raw-ceiling damage march. All three have positive Cavalry affinity. Kalspire's Vanguard package supplies broad stats and protects the right flank, where Sheepstealer is placed. Sheepstealer's 2★ Stolen Flock introduces scaling Fire damage and punishes enemy Recovery. Sunfyre supplies frontline pressure and becomes more dangerous as the fight develops.

This march has less explicit team Recovery than March 1, which is why it ranks below the more complete survival cores despite its very high offensive ceiling.

### March 5 — Vaeldra / Rhysarion / Caraxes

Vaeldra's 4★ state, Dragon's Valor 2 and Ensnare 1 provide a developed control/frontline package. Rhysarion's Ebbing Fury is now rank 3, so this march has a meaningful survival/tempo layer rather than being only a Caraxes carrier. Rhysarion takes Vanguard and Caraxes takes the right flank to receive the Vanguard damage bonus while remaining protected long enough to deliver its multi-target Fire command.

## Marches 6–10

March 6 deliberately keeps the heavily developed Rare trio together. Shimmer is 6★ with Loyal Shield 3, Thunderstrike has Battle Rush 4, and Arulix has two active habits. This is exactly the kind of formation that would be underrated by a plan built only from rarity or public max-level ranks.

March 7 uses Syrax to connect Tashix's Fire scaling with Daemoros's Burn/Physical pressure. March 8 concentrates control effects rather than raw burst. March 9 uses three developed disruption kits around Tairax's strong base control command. March 10 preserves full Cavalry affinity while combining Feskar's suppression, Vermax's strong base ceiling and Shadowsong's current 4★ Ensnare/Fire package.

## Bench for this ten-march set

- Solstryker — useful 4★ depth, but displaced by stronger full-affinity control/damage combinations.
- Antares — good current habit investment, but its overall kit remains narrow and the Archer slots are stronger elsewhere.
- Bevlorin — 4★ defensive utility, but the Spearmen teams above have better combined damage and survival.
- Starshower — strong long-term B-tier utility, but only 1★ today.
- Tessarion — useful offensive support and a good future promotion candidate, but only 1★ today and crowded out by more developed Cavalry/Spearmen options.

## Validation rule going forward

Do not lock any of these formations because of this document alone. The correct next evidence is **new battle data from this September roster state**. When battle reports are added, compare troop losses, damage dealt, enemy power, troop counter, remaining troop percentage and fight duration. Only then should a formation earn a documented 'proven' or 'locked' label.
